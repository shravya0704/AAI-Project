# Sugarcane Irrigation Guidance

## Purpose and limits

This local prototype uses a small, curated knowledge base for irrigation questions
only. It is educational guidance, not a farm-specific prescription. Irrigation
decisions should also consider local soil water-holding capacity, rooting depth,
weather forecasts, field drainage, crop stage, and advice from an agricultural
professional.

## Field observations

Useful observations include soil moisture, recent rainfall, evapotranspiration,
temperature, crop growth stage, days since the last irrigation, soil type, and
the irrigation method. Measurements should be taken consistently at comparable
depths and locations. A single sensor reading should be checked against the wider
field before making a large irrigation decision.

## Interpreting water stress

Low soil moisture combined with high evaporative demand, little recent rainfall,
and a long interval since irrigation can indicate increasing irrigation need.
High recent rainfall or adequate soil moisture can reduce the need for immediate
irrigation, but poor drainage can create a different risk. Crop stage matters:
establishment and grand-growth periods generally deserve closer monitoring than
a field approaching harvest.

## Irrigation advisory principles

An advisory should distinguish between irrigation recommended, monitor and
reassess, and irrigation not currently recommended. The advisory should explain
which observed conditions influenced it. It should not claim an exact irrigation
duration or water volume unless a separately validated model predicts that target.

## Method considerations

Drip irrigation can deliver water near the root zone and may improve application
control. Sprinkler and flood systems have different application efficiencies and
should be evaluated using the actual field system. The method alone does not
determine whether irrigation is needed.

## Safety and uncertainty

The model prediction is a decision-support signal, not a guarantee. Inputs far
outside the prototype training range should not be treated as reliable. When
measurements conflict, rainfall, soil moisture, drainage, and direct crop
observations should be rechecked before irrigation is scheduled.
